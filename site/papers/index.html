GOTV versus Persuasion in U.S. Battleground States

2026

An agent-based campaign model examining when turnout mobilization outperforms persuasion across U.S. battleground states and how network structure changes the optimal strategy. The work models partisan and swing communities under different connectivity regimes and compares mobilization mixes across states. Current results suggest there is no universal GOTV-versus-persuasion optimum; strategy depends on electorate composition and cross-group exposure.

Sacrifice for the State: Identification or Disillusionment?

Ongoing

A historical political-behavior research study examining whether wartime sacrifice strengthens identification with the state or produces later political disillusionment. The work links county-level World War I casualty exposure to turnout, party shifts, bond purchases, Census measures, and related outcomes while preserving uncertainty from archival service records.

Autonomous Agent-Driven Analysis of Federal Sentencing Disparities

2025

What explains persistent disparities in federal sentencing outcomes across race, citizenship status, and socioeconomic position? This paper evaluates an LLM-driven autonomous database agent on more than 60,000 FY2024 U.S. Sentencing Commission cases. The system combines structured reasoning, read-only SQL, and retrieval-assisted schema interpretation to study citizenship, race, education, incarceration, and fines, while keeping the analysis auditable and reviewable by humans.

Why Has International Cooperation on AI Regulation Been Difficult Between the EU and the United States?

2025

International cooperation on artificial intelligence (AI) regulation between the European Union and the United States faces two fundamental structural barriers. The paper identifies an information and enforcement problem created by opaque AI systems, alongside a domestic political divide between U.S. innovation-first preferences and EU precautionary, rights-based governance. It argues binding transatlantic regulation is unlikely soon and cooperation will remain concentrated in soft-law mechanisms, shared standards, and technical coordination.

How Successful Was India's Foreign Policy Under Jawaharlal Nehru During the Cold War?

2025

This paper evaluates the success of India's foreign policy under Jawaharlal Nehru during the Cold War. Through Kashmir, the 1962 Sino-Indian War, and relations with the United States and Soviet Union, it assesses non-alignment as a strategy for autonomy and ethical diplomacy. The argument is mixed: Nehru preserved strategic room and gained selective support from both blocs, but diplomatic posture alone proved insufficient when hard-power preparedness became decisive.

How Effectively Do LLM-Driven, Micro-Tailored Political Messages Influence Public Opinion?

2025

The emergence of large language models (LLMs) as tools for automated political messaging represents a qualitative shift. Comparing LLM microtargeting with earlier algorithmic influence systems, the paper argues the democratic risk is broader than whether tailored messages outperform generic persuasion. It highlights scalable reach, opacity over identity and reasoning, and amplified demographic exploitation as structural harms that can intensify computational persuasion even when personalization itself yields inconsistent marginal gains.

What Explains Variation in State Compliance with the Laws of Armed Conflict Under Conditions of Low Reciprocity?

2025

This paper explains variation in compliance with the Laws of Armed Conflict (LOAC) under asymmetric conditions where reciprocity is weak or absent. Comparing the Gulf War, Israel-Hezbollah War, and Russo-Ukrainian War, it argues international reputation can substitute for material reciprocity: actors reliant on legitimacy, allies, or external assistance tend toward greater restraint, while insulated actors can use violations instrumentally. It also identifies a limit when security costs outweigh expected political penalties.
