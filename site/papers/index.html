Autonomous Agent-Driven Analysis of Federal Sentencing Disparities

2025

What explains persistent disparities in federal sentencing outcomes across race, citizenship status, and socioeconomic position, and can autonomous artificial intelligence systems meaningfully uncover these patterns within complex administrative data? This study evaluates an LLM-driven autonomous database agent on more than 60,000 FY2024 U.S. Sentencing Commission cases, combining structured reasoning, read-only SQL, and retrieval-assisted schema interpretation to surface reproducible patterns in federal punishment.

Why Has International Cooperation on AI Regulation Been Difficult Between the EU and the United States?

2025

International cooperation on artificial intelligence regulation between the European Union and the United States faces two fundamental structural barriers: an information and enforcement problem created by opaque AI systems, and a domestic political divide between U.S. innovation-first preferences and EU precautionary, rights-based governance. The paper argues that binding transatlantic regulation is unlikely in the near term and cooperation will remain concentrated in soft-law mechanisms and technical coordination.

How Successful Was India's Foreign Policy Under Jawaharlal Nehru During the Cold War?

2025

This paper evaluates the success of India's foreign policy under Jawaharlal Nehru during the Cold War, focusing on nonalignment for strategic autonomy and ethical diplomacy for peaceful coexistence. Through Kashmir, the 1962 Sino-Indian War, and relations with the United States and Soviet Union, it finds that Nehru preserved meaningful autonomy and gained selective support from both blocs, while remaining constrained when hard-power preparedness became decisive.

How Effectively Do LLM-Driven, Micro-Tailored Political Messages Influence Public Opinion?

2025

The emergence of large language models as tools for automated political messaging represents a qualitative shift in computational persuasion. Comparing LLM microtargeting with earlier algorithmic influence systems, the paper examines evidence that generated messages can move political opinion while personalized targeting does not consistently outperform generic messaging, and considers the resulting risks for democratic fairness, opacity, and scalable persuasion.

What Explains Variation in State Compliance with the Laws of Armed Conflict Under Conditions of Low Reciprocity?

2025

This paper explains variation in compliance with the Laws of Armed Conflict under asymmetric conditions where reciprocity is weak or absent. Comparing the Gulf War, Israel-Hezbollah War, and Russo-Ukrainian War, it argues that international reputation can substitute for material reciprocity when actors depend on legitimacy, allies, or external assistance, while reputational incentives weaken when perceived security costs outweigh expected political penalties.
